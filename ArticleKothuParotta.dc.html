Kothu parotta is Tamil Nadu flatbread shredded and chopped on a hot griddle with egg, gravy, onion and masala. Kothu means chopped. We cook veg, egg, chicken and mutton versions to order in Toongabbie.

What is kothu parotta, and why is it chopped on the tawa?

If you have walked past a Madurai food street at night, you have heard kothu parotta before you have seen it — two flat blades hitting a hot griddle in a fast, steady rhythm. Kothu means chopped. Parotta is the flaky, layered flatbread of Tamil Nadu. Put them together and you get leftover-parotta alchemy: bread shredded into ribbons, thrown in with egg, gravy, onion and masala, and worked until every strand is coated.

The chopping is not showmanship. Cutting the parotta on the griddle instead of in a bowl means the bread keeps taking on heat as it breaks down, so the edges crisp while the middle stays soft and soaked with gravy. Do it off the heat and you get a damp pile; do it on the tawa and you get texture in every mouthful.

We make four versions in Toongabbie — veg, egg, chicken and mutton. Egg kothu parotta is the traditional late-night one. Mutton is the richest. All of them come with extra gravy on the side, because in Madurai nobody eats kothu parotta dry.

One note for delivery orders: kothu parotta travels better than almost anything else on our menu. It is meant to steam a little in its own heat.
