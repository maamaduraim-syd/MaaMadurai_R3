Seeraga Samba: the small rice that makes Madurai biryani different

Most biryani in Sydney is made with long-grain basmati: separate, fragrant, elegant. Southern Tamil Nadu does something else. Seeraga Samba is a tiny, cumin-seed-shaped grain — the name literally comes from seeragam, cumin — and it behaves nothing like basmati.

It absorbs far more liquid for its size, which means it drinks up mutton stock, ghee and masala instead of sitting beside them. The result is denser, stickier and much more strongly flavoured: a biryani you eat with a spoon and feel in your chest. It also takes longer to cook and punishes impatience, which is why it is a special-occasion rice back home.

Our Seeraga Samba mutton biryani is the dish we would put in front of someone who says they have already tried biryani. Bone-in mutton, slow-simmered, no shortcuts. If you prefer the lighter style, our Madurai chicken biryani uses long-grain rice and is the more familiar plate of the two.
